# Source evaluation: New Advent

- **Candidate:** [New Advent](https://www.newadvent.org/)
- **Publisher / host:** New Advent LLC (founded by Kevin Knight; site online since 1995)
- **Evaluated as:** access hub over several distinct collections, not a single theological source
- **Registry status:** mixed; see per-collection conclusions below
- **Evaluation status:** draft / project-use judgment; theological review pending
- **Rights determination date:** 2026-08-13

## Summary

New Advent is one of the most widely used English-language Catholic reference sites. It is valuable to Catholic Context as a **discovery and access hub**, especially for older public-domain editions of the *Catholic Encyclopedia*, the Church Fathers, and the *Summa Theologiae*.

It is **not** itself a source of Catholic teaching. The homepage mixes encyclopedia articles, patristic texts, the *Summa*, Scripture, a document library, and contemporary news/commentary. Those collections have different source types, editions, translators, and copyright statuses. Catholic Context must cite the underlying work and edition, not "New Advent" as the authority.

Do not scrape or commit the site wholesale. New Advent LLC copyrights the website presentation, and several hosted translations remain third-party copyrighted even where the underlying historical work is public domain.

New Advent is a Catholic website that hosts:

| Collection | Typical URL | Underlying work | Catholic Context source type | Hub role |

| Church Fathers | `/fathers/` | Mostly ANF / NPNF English translations of patristic and related texts | `church-father`, sometimes `doctor-of-the-church`, `ecumenical-council`, or `other` | Convenient access to public-domain translations |

The site also sells a paid digital download of "the full contents of the New Advent website." That commercial packaging is further evidence that the compilation and presentation should be treated as New Advent's product, distinct from the rights and authority of the underlying works.

## Collection evaluations

### 1. Catholic Encyclopedia (1907–1913)

**Identity.** A 15-volume English Catholic encyclopedia published 1907–1913, with a 1914 index and later supplements. New Advent hosts a volunteer transcription begun in the 1990s.

**Authority posture.** Historical Catholic scholarship with ecclesiastical imprimatur for the original print edition. It is not Magisterium, not the Catechism, and not a substitute for post-conciliar teaching. Articles can be excellent for persons, institutions, history, liturgy, and older doctrinal vocabulary. They can also be dated on historical, scientific, political, and ecumenical questions, and they predate Vatican II, the 1983 Code of Canon Law, and the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*.

**Rights.** The original 1907–1913 text is treated here as public domain in the United States because it was published in the US before 1928. New Advent's HTML, navigation, and site compilation remain third-party copyrighted. A modern reprint, scan, or transcription is not automatically free to relicense as CC BY 4.0 merely because the 1913 text is public domain. Document the edition before any bulk inclusion.

**Recommended use.**

- Accept for citation as `scholarship` when the 1913 encyclopedia is the intended source.
- Use it for entity discovery, historical context, and older terminology.
- Prefer primary magisterial, conciliar, catechetical, or canonical sources when the claim is about what the Church teaches now.
- Record the article title, the 1907–1913 encyclopedia edition, and an access URL. Do not treat New Advent as the author.

**Ingestion posture.** Metadata, citations, and original Catholic Context summaries by default. Full-text inclusion of a public-domain article requires an explicit rights determination for the specific transcription/edition being copied, not a general "New Advent is PD" assumption.

**Risks.**

- Readers and models may treat a 1913 encyclopedia article as current Church teaching.
- Some articles contain period historiography that would now be judged inadequate or harmful.
- The 1922 supplement and later *New Catholic Encyclopedia* editions are different works with different rights.

### 2. Church Fathers

**Identity.** A large English corpus of ante-Nicene, Nicene, and post-Nicene writings, plus some liturgies, councils, apocrypha, and miscellaneous texts. New Advent generally hosts the 19th-century *Ante-Nicene Fathers* and *Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers* translations (ed. Roberts, Donaldson, Schaff, and others), not a new Catholic Context translation.

**Authority posture.** High value. These are among the most important non-scriptural sources for Catholic Context `church-father` and `doctor-of-the-church` objects. New Advent usefully labels some works as spurious, local rather than ecumenical, apocryphal, or associated with later heretical movements. That labeling is editorial help, not itself Magisterium.

A Father or Doctor being present in the corpus does not make every proposition in every work definitive Catholic teaching. Spurious, apocryphal, and non-Catholic texts in the same library must not be cited as Church teaching.

**Rights.** The 19th-century ANF/NPNF translations are treated here as public domain in the United States. New Advent's online edition/presentation is copyrighted by New Advent LLC. Later or alternative translations must be assessed separately.

**Recommended use.**

- Accept the underlying patristic works for citation.
- Identify author, work, book/chapter where applicable, and translation/edition.
- A New Advent URL may be stored as an access URL.
- Prefer a more specific source type than `other` whenever the work is genuinely patristic, conciliar, or by a Doctor.

**Ingestion posture.** Same as other public-domain historical translations: cite first; copy text only with a documented edition-level rights determination. Do not ingest New Advent's HTML chrome, commentary, or paid compilation.

**Risks.**

- Translation quality and 19th-century editorial frames vary.
- The library mixes Fathers, Doctors, historians, apocrypha, and spurious works.
- Citing "New Advent, Church Fathers" hides the actual author and edition.

### 3. Summa Theologiae

**Identity.** St. Thomas Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*, English translation by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province, second and revised edition, 1920. New Advent's online edition carries a 2017 copyright notice by Kevin Knight for the online edition, together with the historical nihil obstat / imprimatur notices of the print translation.

**Authority posture.** High value as `doctor-of-the-church`. The *Summa* is central for moral theology, sacraments, and classical doctrinal vocabulary. It remains theological science and sacred doctrine in the Thomistic sense, not a substitute for the Magisterium or the Catechism. Where later magisterial teaching develops, qualifies, or settles a question, cite that teaching rather than treating the *Summa* as final.

**Rights.** The 1920 Dominican Fathers translation is treated here as public domain in the United States. The New Advent online edition/presentation is third-party copyrighted.

**Recommended use.** Cite Aquinas by part, question, and article (`ST` I, I-II, II-II, III, or Supplement). New Advent may be an access URL. Do not cite New Advent as the author.

**Ingestion posture.** Metadata and citations by default. Any full-text use must name the 1920 Dominican translation specifically and must not copy New Advent's site compilation as if it were that translation's rights status.

### 4. Holy Bible (Knox, Vulgate, Septuagint)

**Identity.** A three-column Bible presentation. The English column on the evaluated pages is the Knox translation (re-typeset by Baronius Press; copyright notice: Knox Translation Copyright © 2013 Westminster Diocese). Latin and Greek columns appear alongside it.

**Authority posture.** Scripture remains `scripture` regardless of host. The Knox translation is a Catholic English translation, not the Church's only or current ordinary translation.

**Rights.** Knox is **third-party copyrighted**. Do not ingest or redistribute it. Latin Vulgate and Greek Septuagint rights depend on the specific edition New Advent is displaying and are `unknown` until that edition is identified. Do not infer that those columns are public domain merely because the biblical books themselves are ancient.

**Recommended use.**

- Scripture citations remain `scripture` and should record book, chapter, and verse independently of this host.
- Do not treat New Advent as Catholic Context's canonical Bible edition.
- If a knowledge object needs a Scripture access URL, prefer an authorized edition whose rights are known.
- Do not quote or store Knox text in the repository.

**Ingestion posture.** Never ingest the Knox text. Do not bulk-copy the parallel columns until each edition's rights are documented.

### 5. Catholic Library (Church documents)

**Identity.** A large HTML library of encyclicals, apostolic letters, conciliar texts, CDF documents, and related materials, with coverage especially strong through the late 20th century.

**Authority posture.** The underlying documents may be `magisterium` or `ecumenical-council`. New Advent is a secondary host. For modern documents, an authorized Holy See or conference edition is the better canonical destination.

**Rights.** Mixed and often `unknown` at the translation layer. Older English translations may be public domain; many 20th- and 21st-century English translations are third-party copyrighted. Hosting on New Advent does not grant Catholic Context redistribution rights.

**Recommended use.**

- Accept as a discovery index and, when necessary, as a secondary access URL.
- Prefer `vatican.va` or another authorized source URL in knowledge objects.
- Identify the document by authoring authority, title, date, and section/paragraph, not by the New Advent page title alone.

**Ingestion posture.** Citations, identifiers, and original summaries only, unless a specific document/translation has a documented rights determination.

### 6. News and commentary

**Identity.** The homepage currently foregrounds contemporary Catholic news, syndicated columns, and pastoral commentary.

**Authority posture.** Journalism and opinion. This collection must not ground Catholic Context teaching, discipline, or historical claims.

**Rights.** Third-party copyrighted.

**Recommended use.** `do-not-ground-doctrine`. Linking to a news item for current-events context in documentation is possible; citing it from a knowledge object as if it were a Catholic source is not.

**Ingestion posture.** Never.

## Cross-cutting rules for Catholic Context

1. **Cite the work, not the hub.** A knowledge-object source entry should name the encyclopedia article, patristic work, *Summa* question, biblical pericope, or magisterial document.
2. **Keep source type tied to the underlying work.** New Advent does not become `magisterium` because it hosts encyclicals, and it does not become `church-father` because it hosts Augustine.
3. **Record edition and translator.** Especially for Fathers, the *Summa*, and Scripture.
4. **Prefer authorized destinations for living magisterial texts.** Use New Advent when it is the practical access copy of a public-domain edition, not as a replacement for the Holy See.
5. **Do not confuse imprimatur, popularity, or longevity with current doctrinal authority.**
6. **Do not scrape the site into the repository.** Follow `docs/SOURCE_RIGHTS.md`.

## Conclusion

**Accept New Advent as a related access hub. Accept selected hosted collections for citation with edition-level caveats. Do not accept New Advent itself as a teaching source, and do not ingest the site.**

Highest-value collections for Catholic Context, in order:

1. Church Fathers translations (as access to patristic works)
2. *Summa Theologiae* (Dominican 1920 translation)
3. *Catholic Encyclopedia* 1907–1913 (historical scholarship only)
4. Document library (discovery / secondary access)

Do not use the news feed to ground teaching. Do not ingest the Knox Bible text; cite Scripture by book, chapter, and verse from an edition whose rights are known.
