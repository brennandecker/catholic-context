# Catholic Context Governance

## Purpose

Catholic Context exists to faithfully represent Catholic teaching in an open, source-grounded, machine-readable form. The project does not determine Catholic doctrine.

## Founding governance principles

### 1. Truth is not determined by vote

Votes, reactions, reputation, usage, and popularity may help prioritize work or evaluate clarity and usefulness. They do not determine whether a proposition is Catholic teaching.

### 2. Sources and theological review govern doctrinal representation

Material represented as Catholic teaching must be grounded in appropriate sources and pass the review process applicable to its classification.

### 3. Review status must be transparent

Users and downstream systems must be able to distinguish drafts, community-reviewed material, and theologically reviewed material.

### 4. Commercial independence

Commercial products may fund Catholic Context and may build proprietary experiences on top of it. Commercial considerations must not determine the theological conclusions represented by the open context layer.

### 5. Theological governance

The project intends to establish an independent theological governance function composed of appropriately qualified reviewers. Its responsibilities should include:

- theological review standards
- source and authority standards
- disputed-content review
- doctrinal classification guidance
- reviewer qualification standards
- escalation procedures

Until that body and its procedures are established, theological content should be treated conservatively and clearly marked according to its actual review state.

### 6. Financial sustainability

The project may be supported by commercial applications, managed infrastructure, donations, grants, sponsorships, or other lawful funding. The project intends for a defined portion of sustainable revenue to support theological governance, source verification, and stewardship of the open context layer.

## Founder authority during the founding stage

During the founding stage, the repository owner administers project infrastructure and establishes initial processes. Repository ownership alone does not confer theological authority. As the project matures, theological decision-making should be delegated according to published governance rules.

## Amendments

Material governance changes should be proposed transparently through version-controlled changes and public discussion.
